Abstract
We study the extinction-normalized absorption difference of opposite time-reversed plane
waves incident on a finite compact reciprocal Maxwell scatterer. For every fixed vacuum frequency
and every prescribed upper permittivity and loss capsP > 1 and Q >0, we prove that the
supremum of Γ = |σabs,+−σabs,−|/σext is one, already within local, nonmagnetic, piecewise
homogeneous anisotropic dielectric bodies withI⪯ReHε⪯PI and 0≺ImHε⪯QI. Every body
is compact and every coefficient finite; the sequence has no common size or total material-resource
bound. The value one is unattainable by any individual regular compact object with positive
extinction. The main analytic step proves that cross sections of finite-aperture truncations of a
fixed strictly absorbing planar stack, divided by aperture area, converge to the exact planar values.
A volume integral equation supplies aperture-independent coercivity, and mixed Fourier estimates
control the grazing-radiation singularity. All-angle scattering follows from the exact optical
theorem. For square apertures we give a nonoptimalO((kR)−1/3) convergence estimate at fixed
material parameters. We also derive the sharp complete electric–magnetic dipole cross-section
bound, provide exact radiation-damped equality tensors, and test finite objects with a full-vector
retarded discrete-dipole solver. Known dipolar and finite-plate precedents are distinguished from
the present ordinary mathematical proof. Priority is not certified, and no Lean claim is made in
this phase.

3 Finite-aperture localization and compact dielectric realizations
This section passes from an exact planar dielectric construction to ordinary compact three-
dimensional Maxwell scatterers. The decisive estimate is a volume-current estimate for each
fixed strictly absorbing stack. Scattered power is then obtained from the complete optical theorem,
so all radiation directions, polarization conversion, and the forward diffraction peak remain included.
The argument is analytical; no finite-dimensional matrix completion or numerical convergence
assumption enters it. No claim of priority is made.

7 Implications for OQ7 and remaining obligations
For the question stated in Theorem 1.1, there is a definite answer: no uniform numberc < 1
bounds Γ across all compact local reciprocal dielectrics of unrestricted size, even with fixed positive
frequency and fixed upper real-permittivity and loss caps. Equality is approached by finite objects
and is unattainable at positive extinction. This closes the normalized resource-free compact-body
supremum problem posed for this phase.
It does not establish a complete catalogue of the resource-dependent bounds suggested by the
broader original OQ7. The following remain open here:
1. Sharp laws at fixed diameter, volume, thickness, aperture, integrated loss, or a prescribed
positive tensor-loss floor.
2. A sharp cost of approachingΓ = 1. The proved aperture rate is an upper estimate for each
fixed regularized stack, not an optimal global resource law.
3. The supremum at prescribed nonzero total extinction, particularly with a bounded footprint,
and finite-bandwidth dispersion constraints.
4. An exact local finite-particle realization theorem for every radiation-consistent dipolar equality
tensor, including uniform control of resonant small-sphere asymptotics. This is unnecessary
for the large-aperture counterexample, but remains an independent realization question.
5. Certified finite-mesh continuum error bars for the particular DDA geometries, and a full
prior-theorem comparison sufficient for priority.
The general normalized supremum, the optical theorem, and the all-angle localization limit have
ordinary mathematical proofs in this report. No Lean formalization was attempted, no prior Lean
source was changed, and no existing formal theorem is cited as proving these new analytic steps.
