Abstract
For any prescribed positive plane-wave extinction cross sectionE∗, we prove that the supremum
of the extinction-normalized absorption difference of opposite time-reversed illuminations is one. This
holds for finite compact nonmagnetic reciprocal local anisotropic dielectric bodies, already consisting
of two homogeneous layers, at a fixed frequency and with arbitrary fixed pointwise upper bounds
P >1 on real permittivity andQ> 0 on loss. Every selected body has strictly positive tensor loss,
satisfiesX+ =X− =E∗ exactly, and hasΓ < 1; a sequence approaches one. Its size and material
resources are unrestricted. The new step beyond the preceding compact-object manuscript is uniform
aperture localization after an exact factorization by the squared sine of a physical material-rotation
angle. This allows the angle to decrease inversely with aperture while total extinction stays fixed. A
second intermediate-value argument restores a full positive loss tensor without changing the target
extinction. For the different problem with an enclosing-radius bound,∥χ∥≤M and ImHχ⪰mI >0,
we also prove a uniform sixth-order remainder and hencesup Γ = C∗(m,M )(kR)4 +Om,M((kR)6),
where C∗ is an explicit jointly realizable electrostatic variational supremum. A reciprocal source
Gram bound improves its upper estimate toC∗≤4M 3(1 +M)/(9
√
5m2). The numerical value of
C∗ and the exact finite-resource optimum remain unresolved. No claim of scientific priority or formal
proof-assistant verification is made.

1 Question, scope, and the stronger closure statement

8 Prior work, independent audit, and the exact remaining boundary
Extinction symmetry is established by Sounas and Alù [2]; neither that identity nor asymmetric
absorption by reciprocal objects is new. Hopkins and coauthors [3] and the direct small-particle
expansions discussed in the supplied adversarial audit [13] predate the unequal-sphere mechanism and
fixed-smooth-shape quartic upper order. The present manuscript does not rebrand those effects as
discoveries.
There are also close layered and metasurface precedents. The reciprocal polarizer component of
Kononchuk et al. [8], Sections II–III, has strongly different absorption and reflection from opposite sides;
finite-wafer measurements use Gaussian beams. Its polarization extinction ratio is not the dimensional
optical-theorem extinctionX used here. Máñez-Espina et al. [9], Section 4, derive a maximum directional-
absorptance difference in a specified two-port coupled-mode metasurface model without polarization
conversion. These results do not state Theorem 1.1. A scalar plate with transmission tending to zero and
absorptance difference tending to one has planar extinction density tending to two in the present vacuum
reference, and thereforeΓ tending to 1/2. The polarization construction and normalized finite-aperture
limit address a different extremal ratio.
Shared-structure constraints also have explicit precedents. Molesky et al. [4], Section II.3, equations
(16)–(19), derive off-diagonal source constraints and discuss pointwise enforcement of one geometry.
Shim et al. [5] formulate related cross-correlation constraints for multifunctional response. The source
Gram inequality used here is a standard positive-operator distinguishability estimate, applied to the
exact Maxwell response metric. Its matrix lemma is not claimed new.
The mathematical additions relative to the six supplied manuscripts are: the uniformnormalized
aperture limit, exact positive-extinction tuning and restoration of full tensor loss; and the uniform
optimal small-size variational asymptotic with tighter coefficient bounds. The former closes an explicitly
outstanding issue of [10]. The latter strengthens [12] but leaves its coefficient optimization genuinely
infinite-dimensional.
Two independent AI reviewers rederived the new prescribed-extinction argument, including the
zero-angle normalized problem, grazing-circle estimate, inverse identity(38), and both intermediate-value
steps. A separate independent check rederived the source Gram and coefficient bounds; the parent
independently recalculated the sixth-order constants and volume cancellation. The audit notes and
numerical scripts are preserved. These are ordinary mathematical audits, not proof-assistant certificates
or independent human peer review. Literature priority is not established by failure to locate an identical
theorem.
