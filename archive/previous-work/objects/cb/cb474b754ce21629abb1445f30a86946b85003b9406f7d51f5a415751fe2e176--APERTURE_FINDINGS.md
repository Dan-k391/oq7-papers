## Finding A1: explicitly qualify grazing fibers

- **Severity:** Low; definitional precision, not a counterexample or obstruction.
- **Location:** PDF pp. 8–9, (32)–(36); source lines 299–323, 328–343.
- **Problem:** (32) writes `i/(2β)` at every transverse wavevector, including `|κ|=k`, where it is undefined. Later operator norm and integral expressions implicitly use the fibers only almost everywhere. The global transform itself must be understood as the outgoing limiting-absorption distribution, not as an ordinary pointwise value on the circle.
- **Repair:** State: “The following formulas hold for almost every κ, excluding `|κ|=k`. The Weyl representation is the distributional outgoing boundary value. Assign any measurable value to the fiber on the grazing circle when evaluating Lebesgue integrals; this does not change the representation or the estimates.” For maximal explicitness, derive the Weyl identity for `k+iτ`, `τ>0`, then take its distributional boundary value as `τ↓0`; the displayed locally integrable branch `i/(2√(k²-|κ|²))` is that limit. Do not replace this with a global L2 claim.

## Checks and independent derivations

### Rank-one reduction, exact factorization, and zero angle

PDF pp. 5–6, (16)–(22); source lines 177–221.

The constitutive operator is precisely `χ=JθαJθ*`, with `Jθ` a real pointwise isometry. Hence a physical current is `Jθq`; its equation is `(α⁻¹-Jθ*K_DJθ)q=Jθ*f`. Since the loss weight is `w=diag_z(1/η, δ/(a²+δ²))`, radiation nonnegativity gives the stated coercivity independently of both aperture and angle. It is not a coercivity claim for the total electric field in the loss-free null directions.

For the fixed unit-amplitude y-polarized plane waves, `Jθ*f±=sinθ g±` exactly. The scalar equation is linear, so `q=sinθ r` and `p=sinθ Jr`. Extinction therefore gains one sine from the source projection and one from the current; absorption and scattering are quadratic in the current. All three `sin²θ` factorizations in (21) are exact. The reciprocity calculation applies to `g−=conj(g+)`, so the normalized extinction is common in the two directions as well.

At θ=0 the physical incident waves induce **zero current** and zero physical powers. The nonzero `r_R,0` is the separately defined response to the artificial scalar drive `g`; it is not a physical zero-angle scattering state. Lines 206 and 282 make this distinction correctly. Division of physical zero powers is never needed.

### Planar phase, polarization conversion, and reflected channels

PDF pp. 6–8 and p. 14, (23)–(28), (50)–(51); source lines 229–274 and 473–489.

The active principal index is `n=2j/(2j-1)`, so its plate phase is `2jπ`; the inactive index is one, with phase `(2j-1)π`. The boundary-plane plate transfer matrix is therefore `2uuᵀ-I₂`, whereas its vacuum reference phase is **−1**. This distinction produces the relative co-polar transmission `cos2θ`, not its negative.

I derived the cascades independently using

`R_A=r_A xxᵀ`, `T_A=diag(t_A,v_A)`, `R_W=r_W uuᵀ`, `T_W=v_W I+Δ uuᵀ`.

For + incidence, the right-going internal field is

`d=(I-R_A R_W)⁻¹ v_A y = v_A[y+(r_A r_W cs/D)x]`, `D=1-r_A r_W c²`.

Its reflected output is `T_A R_W d`, and its transmitted output is `T_W d`. Subtracting `v_Av_W y` from the transmission and dividing all scattered components by `s` gives exactly (50), including both reflected polarizations.

For − incidence, the left-going internal field is

`l=(I-R_W R_A)⁻¹ T_W y`, with `l_x=Δsc/D` and `l_y=v_W+Δs²/D`.

The left output is `T_A l`; the right reflection is `R_W y+T_W R_A l`. This gives exactly (51), including the `r_AΔ(v_W+Δc²)/D` and `r_AΔ²c²/D` feedback terms.

At zero plate loss, `r_W=0`, `t_W=1`, `v_W=-1`, `Δ=2`. Thus the normalized total scattering is `C∞+=4` and `C∞−=4[s²+c²(|r_A|²+|t_A|²)]`. The work formula gives `Y∞=4`, hence `B∞+=0` and `B∞−=4c²A_x`. This includes reflected fields and both transverse output polarizations, and recovers (26).

The planar optical theorem normalization can also be derived without the cited slab reference: the outgoing transmission increment relative to vacuum is `(ik/2) ∫e^{-ikz}p_y(z) dz`; therefore `2(1-Re t_co)=k Im∫e^{-ikz}p_y dz`, the manuscript's work density. The outgoing left and right electric amplitudes are `(ik/2)∫e^{±ikz}p(z)dz`, giving the same scattering density as `k Im⟨p,K_0p⟩`.

The lossless plate limit is regular for each fixed finite absorber. At `(δ,θ)=(0,0)`, `D=1`; all normalized output formulas are continuous in a neighborhood. The source is normalized before passing to the limit. The additional Fredholm/one-dimensional uniqueness argument at line 489 correctly avoids using a coercivity constant that stays positive as δ→0. The asymptotic choice `η=1/N`, `kh=N²` gives `|r_0|→0` and `|p|≈exp(-N/2)→0`, so `A_x→1` within the real-permittivity lower bound.

### Uniform aperture limit and order of limits

PDF pp. 8–10, (29)–(38); source lines 276–355.

The source is globally zero-extended in all three coordinates and the observation field is defined globally on the strip before any transverse Fourier transform. Restricting the observation field after inversion gives the finite-body Green operator. Thus (35) does not mistakenly transform an aperture-restricted field.

For fixed smooth window `v`, the low-frequency estimate follows from

`∥R² v̂(Rκ)(Bκ-B0)r∥ ≤ C R² |v̂(Rκ)| |κ| ∥r∥`.

Hilbert-valued Plancherel makes its global L2 norm `O(∥∇v_R∥₂∥r∥)=O(∥∇v∥₂∥r∥)`, independent of R. The fiber bound is uniform in angle because each J is an isometry.

For `|κ|>k/2`, Schwartz decay yields `R²|v̂(Rκ)|≤C_N R^{2-N}|κ|^{-N}`. The operator growth is at most O(|κ|) at infinity. The radial high-frequency integral therefore behaves as `∫ρ^{2-N}dρ`, finite for `N>3`. At grazing, the `|ρ-k|^{-1/2}` singularity is locally L1. Bochner L1 inversion gives transverse L∞(L2_z) norm `O(R^{2-N}∥r∥)`; restriction to the aperture multiplies it by R, giving `O(R^{3-N}∥r∥)`. This is the right estimate. The global high-frequency field is not required to be L2.

The density passage is legitimate at fixed R: approximate height profiles in L2, use boundedness of the restricted `K_D`, and use the same high-frequency Bochner L1 bound to identify the inverse transform locally. Piecewise constant J may create a height jump but does not obstruct the distributional Fourier identity or L2 density.

The trial-current residual is exactly `(v_R-1)g-H_R`. With `∥L_R⁻¹∥≤c0⁻¹`, this yields (38). Crucially, R tends to infinity **at fixed v**, and only then is v chosen closer to one in L2. Constants involving derivatives or Schwartz seminorms of v may diverge as v approaches the sharp window; the argument does not require them to stay bounded. The material and incident field remain sharp throughout.

Both currents are uniformly O(R), so the normalized extinction and absorption errors are o(1). Scattering is obtained by the exact full-radiation identity and subtraction, rather than by an aperture-dependent bound on the radiation quadratic form. Since positive w and nonzero g imply `Y∞=B∞+C∞>0`, continuity and compactness supply a positive denominator bound. Consequently the normalized aperture limit does include arbitrary angle sequences θ_R→0.

### First extinction bracket

PDF p. 10, (39)–(41); source lines 357–378.

The construction freezes η,h,δ,d before R increases. The endpoints have θ=O(1/R), so the uniform normalized limit gives `Y_R(θ)/R²→Y0`; `sinθ/θ→1` then yields extinction limits E*/2 and 2E*. Strict finite-R bracketing follows. Operator-norm angle continuity at fixed R and IVT give an exact root without a derivative or monotonicity claim. All roots retained in this bracket tend to zero and obey the stated Rθ asymptotic. The normalized asymmetry margin is uniform across the entire bracket, as required for later retuning.

### Full-vector loss perturbation, material caps, and second retuning

PDF p. 11, (42)–(43); source lines 380–398.

The perturbation is exactly `iνI` in both layers, not an active-axis-only loss. The identity in (43) is a valid two-sided Woodbury identity: multiplying either way reduces to `L=α⁻¹-J*KJ`, and it gives the full-vector electric inverse even though χ at ν=0 is rank deficient. Its norm bound is `1+∥K_D∥/c0` for the selected finite R. The Neumann condition `ν∥K_D∥(1+∥K_D∥/c0)<1` then supplies uniform continuity on the fixed compact angle bracket.

The unperturbed extinction minimum on the bracket is positive because every angle there has nonzero sine and `Y_R>0`; thus the ratio is continuous with a nonzero denominator. One ν can preserve both endpoint inequalities and the uniform asymmetry margin. IVT again restores the exact prescribed extinction. The two final tensors have real eigenvalues `{1,1,1}` and `{p_j,1,1}` and imaginary eigenvalues `{η+ν,ν,ν}` and `{δ+ν,ν,ν}`. With each of η,δ,ν below Q/3, they obey strict positive loss and the stated caps. Symmetry and locality are preserved.

The smallness choice for ν is allowed to depend on the already selected finite R and may tend to zero across the sequence. This matches the target's lack of a common loss floor and does not weaken its quantifiers.
