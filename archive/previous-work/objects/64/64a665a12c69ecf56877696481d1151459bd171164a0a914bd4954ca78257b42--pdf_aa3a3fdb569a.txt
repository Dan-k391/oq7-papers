Unit Absorption Asymmetry at Prescribed Extinction
for Compact Reciprocal Dielectrics
Abstract
For two opposite real linearly polarized plane waves, reciprocity ﬁxes the extinction of a com-
pact scatterer but permits diﬀerent absorption and scattering. At a ﬁxed vacuum wavenumber
k > 0, we establish that the supremum of the absorption diﬀerence divided by common extinc-
tion is one at every exactly prescribed extinction E∗ > 0. The realization uses ﬁnite compact
two-layer local anisotropic nonmagnetic dielectrics satisfying arbitrary ﬁxed upper caps P > 1
and Q > 0, with I ⪯ ReH ε ⪯ P I and 0 ≺ ImH ε ⪯ QI in each layer. Every individual body
has asymmetry strictly below one. The constructive step is a physical aperture limit uniform
after factorization by the squared sine of a material rotation angle, including its zero-angle
auxiliary limit. This permits the coupling angle to decrease as the aperture increases while
total extinction remains ﬁxed. A subsequent positive isotropic-loss perturbation and a second
intermediate-value argument preserve the exact target. Geometric resources are unrestricted
across the approximating sequence, and no common positive loss ﬂoor or bandwidth is imposed.
The polarization mechanism and reciprocal extinction identity have prior precedents; the con-
tribution is the controlled compact Maxwell realization at prescribed extinction. A frozen Lean
4 formalization certiﬁes the stated existence, suprema, and physical power identities, with the
scope of its veriﬁcation distinguished from independent semantic and priority review.
1 Introduction and relation to OQ7
Open Question 7 in the tutorial of Miller and Monticone asks for fundamental limits on the redis-
tribution between absorption and scattering under time-reversed illumination [ 1]. A normalization,
illumination class, and resource class are needed to turn that question into a theorem. Here the
normalization is the common dimensional plane-wave extinction, the illumination pair is opposite
real linear polarization in vacuum, and the objects are local reciprocal dielectric bodies at one
frequency.
The result is an existence and supremum theorem. It states that a ﬁnite object can have exactly
any prescribed positive extinction and an absorption diﬀerence arbitrarily close to that extinction,
while its real permittivity and loss obey ﬁxed pointwise upper bounds. It establishes neither a
ﬁnite-resource optimum nor ﬁnite-bandwidth performance. The same material tensor is used in
both experiments, and scattering includes the whole outgoing sphere and both polarizations.
At a ﬁxed coupling angle, enlarging a planar-like aperture increases extinction proportionally
to area. A ﬁxed-stack aperture limit therefore does not suﬃce to prescribe total extinction. The
exact rotation factorization and uniform normalized aperture theorem below permit the coupling
angle to decrease while the aperture grows. Green-operator and localization arguments justify this
passage for actual ﬁnite Maxwell bodies. Dipole optimization, scalar thickness laws, ﬁxed-resource
bounds, and quartic small-size results have diﬀerent hypotheses and are outside the present proof.

2 Prior work and the precise contribution
Reciprocal extinction equality and directional absorption are established facts. Sounas and Alù
derived opposite-incidence extinction symmetry and its implications for scattering manipulation [ 2].
Hopkins and coauthors studied directional absorption and scattering at equal extinction through
modal interference in reciprocal particles [ 3]. The passive inequality used below is a consequence of
power conservation, and its strictness follows from the optical theorem. We claim no independent
discovery of these facts.
Polarization conversion combined with selective attenuation also has a substantial history. Ar-
mitage gives reciprocity constraints for Jones matrices and a polarizer–waveplate example [ 4]. Khur-
gin and coauthors demonstrated reciprocal asymmetric polarization switching using selective at-
tenuation and imperfect waveplates, including passive experiments [ 5]. This is a closely related
channel mechanism. Ra’di, Asadchy, and Tretyakov analyze thin bianisotropic absorbers and dis-
tinct opposite-side responses, distinguishing reciprocal and nonreciprocal constitutive classes [ 6].
Such sheet and channel constructions establish relevant mechanisms; their realizability assump-
tions must be retained when comparing them with local nonmagnetic dielectric volumes. Likewise,
the lossless-response time-reversal bounds of Sounas and Alù concern a diﬀerent set of hypotheses
[7].
Andersen and Frandsen showed that receiving-antenna absorption divided by absorption plus
total scattering can approach one, with a directivity-dependent cost [ 8]. That result precedes any
general near-unit absorption-eﬃciency claim. It does not by itself supply the present simultaneous
opposite-incidence response at every prescribed extinction within a capped local dielectric class.
Schab, Gustafsson, and collaborators formulate absorption–scattering tradeoﬀs with prescribed
losses and material parameters [ 9]; those ﬁnite-resource optimization problems motivate the re-
source distinctions maintained here. Khurgin and Sun’s loss-scaling analysis supplies separate
material and conﬁnement context for resonant metal–dielectric structures [ 10].
Related layered and metasurface devices also require explicit comparison. Kononchuk and coau-
thors combine dichroic attenuation with magnetic Faraday rotation in a layered-sheet isolator [ 11];
its complete device has a diﬀerent, magnetically nonreciprocal constitutive class, while its selective-
attenuation component is relevant. Earlier bianisotropic metasurface asymmetric absorption was
studied by Yazdi and coauthors [ 12]. Máñez-Espina and coauthors derive maximum directional
absorptance diﬀerence in a reciprocal bianisotropic metasurface model and verify its predictions
with full-wave simulations [ 13]. A planar absorptance diﬀerence in a speciﬁed coupled-mode model
is distinct from a compact dimensional extinction-normalized supremum with every positive tar-
get and arbitrary material caps. These comparisons do not establish ﬁrst priority for the latter
theorem.
For the ﬁnite-to-planar passage, Gao, Yang, and Kattawar studied polarized extinction of plates
with large aspect ratios [ 14]. Gustafsson and coauthors derived planar extinction and scattered-
transmission identities for periodic slabs [ 15]; literal compact-material truncation and the uniform
normalized limit required here have diﬀerent quantiﬁers. Windowed Green-function methods rig-
orously treat layered backgrounds [ 16, 17], while volume-integral formulations supply the singular-
kernel and radiation framework [ 18, 19]. A computational window applied to an inﬁnite-interface
equation and literal truncation of a material body are diﬀerent operations. Our smooth windows
enter only trial currents; the ﬁnal material aperture remains sharp.
Mann and Alù’s 2023 conference abstract explicitly announces maximal directional absorption
for bianisotropic dipolar particles [ 20]. It is direct prior art for the qualitative extremal phenomenon.
The full equations, normalization, and any equivalent compact dielectric realization have not been
veriﬁed from that contribution. Priority against it remains unresolved. More generally, absence of

11 Conclusion
The prescribed-extinction problem has a unit normalized supremum within ﬁnite compact two-layer
reciprocal anisotropic dielectrics at one frequency and under arbitrary ﬁxed positive upper material
caps. The proof combines exact rotation factorization, uniform normalized aperture localization,
and two ﬁnite-object continuity arguments. Each selected object retains the exact extinction target
and strictly positive tensor loss, while strict optical-theorem nonattainment prevents equality for
an individual body. This gives a precise resource-unrestricted answer to one formulation of OQ7;
ﬁnite-resource optima and bandwidth remain separate questions.
