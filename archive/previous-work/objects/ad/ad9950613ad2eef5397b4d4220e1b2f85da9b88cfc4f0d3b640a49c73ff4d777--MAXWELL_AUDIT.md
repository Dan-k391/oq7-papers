## Green operator, contact term, and constants

Locations: pp. 3–5, (5)–(7), (11)–(15); lines 82–106 and 129–174.

For the zero extension of `p∈L²(D)³`, the definition is the genuine distributional operator

`K_D p = [(k²I+∇div)(g_k*p)]|D`, `g_k=e^{ikr}/(4πr)`.

The spherical-principal-value static Hessian has the `−Iδ0/3` term: the small-sphere integration-by-parts boundary contribution is fixed by `∫S² n_i n_j dΩ=(4π/3)δ_ij`. Its full-space Fourier symbol is `−ξξᵀ/|ξ|²`, interpreted arbitrarily at the single zero-frequency point. Its L² norm is at most one. This would fail to describe the operator if one silently omitted the contact term; no such omission occurs here.

Independently differentiating the radial kernel gives dynamic radial and tangential eigenvalues `−2b(kr)/(4πr³)` and `[(kr)²e^{ikr}+b(kr)]/(4πr³)`, where `b(u)=e^{iu}(iu−1)+1`. Since `b'(u)=−ue^{iu}`, `|b(u)|≤u²/2`. The complex kernel is diagonal in orthogonal real radial/tangential projections, so its norm is bounded by `3k²/(8πr)`. For a measurable bounded D, rearrangement gives

`sup_x ∫D |x−y|⁻¹ dy ≤ 2π [3|D|/(4π)]^{2/3}`.

Applying Schur to both integration variables yields exactly `(3/4)k²[3|D|/(4π)]^{2/3}` in (12). No boundary smoothness of the aperture is needed.

The imaginary scalar kernel is smooth:

`Im g_k(x) = k/(16π²) ∫S² e^{ik n·x} dΩ`.

Applying the dyadic differential operator supplies `k²(I−nnᵀ)`. Compact L² currents are L¹, so angular/spatial interchange and the quadratic identity are justified. The Hermitian imaginary part of the reciprocal convolution kernel gives

`Im⟨p,K_Dp⟩ = k³/(16π²) ∫S² |Π_n∫D p(y)e^{−ik n·y}dy|² dΩ`.

Thus `F_p=k²/(4π) Π_n∫D p e^{−ik n·y}`, `S=∫S²|F_p|²`, and **`S=k Im⟨p,K_Dp⟩`**. These constants agree. The distributional contact term is real and changes no imaginary work.

At large radius, compact support and L¹ integrability give the uniform electric remainder `E_s(rn)=e^{ikr}F_p(n)/r+O(r⁻²)` and magnetic remainder `H_s(rn)=e^{ikr}n×F_p(n)/r+O(r⁻²)`. The normalized Poynting flux tends to S on the whole sphere. The two transverse polarizations are included by the vector norm, not selected specular channels.

With impedance-normalized magnetic field, incident intensity is `1/(2Z0)`. The same factor in the time-averaged radiated power cancels; dissipation divided by that intensity is `k∫E† Im_Hχ E`. Hence there is no missing factor of two, impedance, or k in X, A, or S. At the incident direction, `e_y†Π_{±e_z}=e_y†`; multiplying the far field by `4π/k` gives exactly `k Im⟨f,p⟩`.

## Weak reconstruction, converse, and entire uniqueness

Locations: p. 3, (1)–(4); p. 5, (14)–(15); pp. 13–14, (47)–(49); lines 48–80, 158–174, 450–471.

For each selected strictly lossy two-tensor body, finite-dimensional strict positivity supplies `Im_Hχ≥mI` and a finite norm bound M. χ is invertible, and

`W=−Im_Hχ⁻¹=χ^{−†}(Im_Hχ)χ⁻¹≥(m/M²)I`.

The sign is correct for time dependence `e^{−iωt}` and an inner product conjugate-linear in its first argument. For `L=χ⁻¹−K`, the real part of `i⟨q,Lp⟩` on the diagonal is `−Im⟨p,Lp⟩≥(m/M²)||p||²`. Complex Lax–Milgram gives a two-sided inverse; this is not an injectivity-only inference.

To expand the compressed reconstruction bridge, put `V=g_k*p`. Cauchy–Schwarz on compact source support, with `g_k∈L²loc`, makes V locally bounded and locally L². Its fundamental-solution identity gives `(-Δ−k²)V=p`. Local scalar elliptic regularity therefore gives `V∈H²loc`. Define

`E=f+k²V+∇div V`, `H=(ik)⁻¹ curl E=−ik curl V+H_inc`.

Then E is locally L² and H is locally H¹. The commuting distributional derivatives and the fundamental-solution equation give `curl E=ikH` and `curl H=−ik(E+p)`. Inside D, the solved volume equation gives `p=χE`; outside D, p vanishes. Thus the weak coefficient is exactly the stated local dielectric tensor, including interface conditions encoded distributionally. Both curls are locally L². Radiation is imposed on **E−f and H−H_inc**, not on the incident-containing total field.

The converse is essential and present. Given any weak scattering state, its compact polarization is locally L² and supported in D. Subtract its reconstructed source fields and the incident fields. The residual solves the vacuum equations on all of R³ and is outgoing. Appendix A proves its vanishing without assuming smooth traces of arbitrary representatives.

The weighted Poynting identity has zero real flux for an entire vacuum pair. Combining it with the outgoing defect gives (48), since `∫w_R/r² dx=4π`. The bilinear Lorentz identity against the outgoing field generated by an arbitrary compact smooth vector q has the displayed sign in (49). Its two boundary terms are bounded using the shell L² estimate, `E_q=O(r⁻¹)`, and the source-field radiation defect `O(r⁻²)`. The needed integrals are `∫w_R/r² dx=4π` and `∫w_R/r⁴ dx≤4π/R²`. Both terms vanish. Every compact test pairing with E is therefore zero, and the curl equation forces H=0 almost everywhere. Local mollification justifies weighted product identities: local L² products converge in L¹ and the constant-coefficient curl equations commute with mollification. No classical trace assumption is inserted into the converse.

The constitutive energy balance follows directly from the solved equation and the complete radiation identity. Reciprocity uses the bilinear, unconjugated transpose pairing and symmetric susceptibility/Green kernel. `f_−=conj f_+` because the polarization is real. The two extinctions consequently coincide on the **same** body.

## Null-sphere counterexample to an overstrong interpretation

This is a counterexample to **raw representative flux**, not to the target or the frozen theorem. Start with an admissible body's canonical weak fields, and choose radii r_j outside it. On each sphere alone, add a transverse vector `a_j(n)` to E and `n×a_j(n)` to H. Leave the fields unchanged elsewhere. The modifications are supported on a countable union of three-dimensional null sets. All weak equations, local L² classes, compact polarization, extinction, and absorption are unchanged. Since `(n×a_j)×n=a_j`, the Silver–Müller defect is also unchanged pointwise. Choosing the amplitudes large can destroy convergence of sphere integrals computed from these raw values.

The canonical reconstruction and trace interpretation remove this freedom. The manuscript explicitly uses that interpretation at line 80, and Lean's sphere-flux quantity uses the reconstructed radiating fields. The ordinary and formal statements are consistent here.
