## 1. Expansion of the material and body definitions

`Basic.lean:14–17` defines

- `Position := EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)`, actual three-dimensional Euclidean space;
- `Vector := EuclideanSpace ℂ (Fin 3)`;
- `Tensor := Matrix (Fin 3) (Fin 3) ℂ`;
- `Field := Position → Vector`.

There is no finite sampling grid or hidden finite-dimensional replacement for the fields. `Basic.lean:24–30` uses matrix-vector multiplication, the Hermitian pairing `Σᵢ conj(uᵢ) vᵢ`, a separate bilinear pairing `Σᵢ uᵢvᵢ`, and `squareNorm v = Σᵢ |vᵢ|²`.

`MaterialAdmissible P Q ε` at `Basic.lean:34–39` expands to

1. `ε.transpose = ε`;
2. for every complex vector `v`, `‖v‖² ≤ Re(v* ε v) ≤ P ‖v‖²`;
3. for every nonzero complex vector `v`, `0 < Im(v* ε v)`;
4. for every complex vector `v`, `Im(v* ε v) ≤ Q ‖v‖²`.

These are precisely transpose reciprocity and the Hermitian quadratic-form bounds `I ≤ Re_H ε ≤ P I` and `0 < Im_H ε ≤ Q I`. They are not bounds on the entrywise real part in a restricted real test space. The strict inequality quantifies over every nonzero **complex** vector. Finite-dimensional positivity implies a body-specific positive coercivity constant; no common constant is imposed across different bodies.

`TwoLayerBody` at `Basic.lean:53–61` consists of three real dimensions `R,h,d`, two complex constant 3×3 tensors, and proofs `R>0,h>0,d>0`. A real dimension is finite; no extended-real infinity is allowed. At `Basic.lean:47–50,63`, its material domain is

`(-R/2,R/2) × (-R/2,R/2) × (0,h+d)`.

`TwoLayerBody.permittivity` at `Basic.lean:66–70` is the absorber tensor in the part with `z<h`, the plate tensor in the rest of the material domain, and `I` outside the domain. `susceptibility` at line 72 is this field minus `I`. Thus the constitutive law is local tensor multiplication, and the exterior is vacuum. Assigning either tensor at the internal interface and vacuum on the outer boundary changes only volume-null sets. The domain itself is open; the physical compactness assertion is compactness of its closure, explicitly proved by `Geometry.lean:26` (`domain_compact_closure`). This is the same convention as manuscript lines 61–67.

`B.admissible P Q` at `Basic.lean:75–77` requires **both** constant tensors to satisfy `MaterialAdmissible`. Nonmagnetism is encoded in the Maxwell equations below, rather than supplied by an unconstrained permeability parameter.

The chosen final material is explicitly anisotropic: `isotropicRankOneBody` (`IsotropicPhysicalBody.lean:8`) uses

`ε_A = I + α e_x e_xᵀ + iν I`,

`ε_W = I + β u_θ u_θᵀ + iν I`, where `u_θ=(cos θ,sin θ,0)`.

`IsotropicPhysicalBody.lean:16–33` verifies the actual susceptibility action `α(u·E)u+iνE` for all three components. This is not a scalar perturbation confined to the active polarization. `IsotropicPhysicalAdmissibility.lean:6` and `Materials.lean:56` prove the final tensor caps.

### S-2 — Sphere flux needs the canonical exterior representative

**Severity:** resolved qualification; no target failure. It would be a substantive error only if raw pointwise flux of every arbitrary weak representative were claimed.

**Location:** `Maxwell.lean:75–82` permits arbitrary pointwise representatives. `ScatteringStatePhysicalRadiation.lean:9–22` deliberately defines its flux from the current-reconstructed radiating fields, and `:49–54` proves only AE equality of the original state with those fields. `FullPhysicalOpticalTheorem.lean:15–23` respects this distinction. The manuscript explicitly states it at lines 80, 432, and 471.

**Counterexample to an overstrong reading:** start with a physical scattering state and select infinitely many spheres outside the body. On those volume-null spheres add a transverse field `a(r,n)` to `E` and `n×a(r,n)` to impedance-normalized `H`; elsewhere change nothing. The weak Maxwell equations, local L² memberships, polarization current, and original powers are unchanged. Moreover `(n×a)×n=a` for `a·n=0`, so the Silver–Müller residual is unchanged pointwise. Choose `a` to grow arbitrarily fast on the selected spheres. The raw flux evaluated from those representatives can then grow arbitrarily along those radii, even though original `S` is unchanged. This is an in-class representative counterexample, **not** a counterexample to the theorem or its canonical flux certificate.

**Repair:** retain the canonical-representative wording in every physical-flux claim. An equivalent stronger state API could require exterior smooth representatives or define sphere flux from exterior traces. The current final mathematical statement already uses the correct approach and needs no weakening.

### S-3 — The unrestricted universal ratio has a zero-extinction convention

**Severity:** low; scope/notation qualification, no prescribed-extinction gap.

**Location:** `normalizedAsymmetry` at `Maxwell.lean:109` is a total real division. `PhysicalTheorem11.lean:24–28` includes universal weak bounds before its explicit positive-extinction implication. `universal_physical_asymmetry_le_one` (`UniversalPhysicalBounds.lean:38–48`) handles zero extinction by simplification of this totalized ratio. Lean assigns `a/0=0`; that is not the physical definition of an extinction-normalized ratio.

**Repair:** document this auxiliary extension or state all physically interpreted ratio clauses under `X_+>0`. The requested theorem already has `X_+=target>0`, both supremum classes have the same positive target, the nonattainment arguments use it, and all required ratio-continuity/cancellation steps establish their nonzero denominators. No target repair is needed.
