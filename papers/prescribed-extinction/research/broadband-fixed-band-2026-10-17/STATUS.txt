BROADBAND FIXED-BAND RESEARCH DRAFT - 17 October 2026

Current result
A proposed constructive proof that the worst-frequency absorption-asymmetry
supremum is one on any fixed finite positive-frequency band, in the base
paper's general homogeneous two-layer reciprocal tensor class, for every
strict extinction floor 0 < E_min < E_star, with exact X(omega_c) = E_star.
Each selected body has one finite passive causal Lorentz-sum material law.
The bandwise real-permittivity and loss upper caps are retained.

Essential assumptions and scope
No common bounds on aperture, thickness, volume, oscillator strengths,
oscillator count, or a positive loss lower bound across the sequence.
Caps apply on the prescribed band. General tensor dispersion is essential
for the all-strict-floor statement; individual oscillator axes remain fixed.
The proof inherits the base manuscript's weak-Maxwell volume representation,
radiation identity, optical theorem, and bounded-domain operator framework.

New analytical steps
1. Approximate a positive conversion target by signed resolvent sums.
2. Complete each signed off-diagonal residue to a positive semidefinite
   matrix, paying for it with a potentially enormous yy entry.
3. Derive normalized weak/thick matrix-slab and selective-absorber limits.
4. Extend the auxiliary-current factorization and aperture localization
   uniformly over a compact positive-frequency band.
5. Use a tight coupling bracket to preserve the entire-band extinction floor,
   add small isotropic loss, and retune only once at the center frequency.

Numerical scope
The exact planar-stack convergence tests use a moderate matrix and sampled
frequencies. Separate eight-pole dispersion shaping evaluates only the ideal
normalized coefficient. It is not a simulation of a shaped finite device.
Independent standing-wave volume absorption is checked for plus incidence.
The algebraic outgoing-amplitude energy residuals are not independent tests.
No finite three-dimensional aperture simulation or interval certificate was run.
