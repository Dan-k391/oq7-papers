7.1 Method and all-channel accounting
We solve the finite-sphere Maxwell boundary problem with regular vector spherical waves inside each
sphere and outgoing vector spherical waves outside. Both electric and magnetic wave families are
retained for every1≤ℓ≤ℓmax. For a coaxial isotropic pair under axial plane waves polarized along
x, axial symmetry confines the solution exactly to azimuthal ordersm =±1. This is a symmetry
reduction, not a dipole truncation.

8 Prior work and scientific positioning
Extinction symmetry is an established reciprocity consequence. Sounas and Alù [2] also studied
unequal lossy gold nanospheres under opposite illumination, including total scattering and extinction.
Thus neither the existence of asymmetric absorption and scattering nor the unequal-sphere example
is claimed as a new physical mechanism. The accessible publisher preview and figure captions
establish that direct antecedent; a complete page-by-page comparison with their unavailable full
derivation remains a priority check before submission.
Heterodimer direction dependence was investigated earlier by Brown et al. [3]. Their measured
and simulated collection-cone spectra over a substrate require care when comparing with a whole-
space vacuum scattering cross section. Hopkins and coauthors studied absorption/scattering
dichroism and its relation to nonorthogonal modes [4, 5]. Their complex-polarization cases also
show why the illumination convention cannot be omitted here.
The need for one shared physical structure across multiple incident fields is established in
T-operator and cross-correlation approaches [6, 7]. The present theorem uses that requirement in
the particularly simple formp+−p−=Tk(f+−f−). Material-response bounds and radiation-to-loss
tradeoffs also predate this work [8, 9]. The preceding OQ7 fixed-resource manuscript already used
such estimates to obtain a cubic small-volume bound when the two currents were relaxed separately.
Small-inclusion full-Maxwell expansions have a substantial literature, including Ammari and
Khelifi [10]. Coupled-dipole optical theorems and radiation corrections are likewise well established
[11, 12]. A point-particle model alone is not a remainder estimate for a finite cluster whose entire
separation shrinks. Here the cluster shape is fixed first, its exact electrostatic moment is certified
second, and a full-operator Maxwell estimate controls its subsequent homothetic size limit.
Mann and Alù’s 2023 conference contribution on maximum asymmetric absorption in bian-
isotropic particles is an additional priority lead [13]. Only its official abstract and program
information were available in this review. Its constitutive class, normalization, and relation to the
present bounded dielectric size limit have not been fully checked.
No matching geometry-uniform quartic theorem was located in the inspected literature. This
