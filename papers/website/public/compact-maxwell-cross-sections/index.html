Abstract

Reciprocity fixes the extinction of time-reversed plane waves but allows their absorption to differ. We study this difference normalized by their common extinction. For a fixed strictly absorbing planar tensor stack, we prove that the extinction, absorption and total scattering cross sections of its compact finite-aperture truncations, divided by aperture area, converge to the exact planar densities. The proof combines an aperture-independent inverse bound for the volume equation with mixed Fourier estimates that retain the grazing-radiation singularity. The optical theorem supplies the all-angle scattering limit. For square apertures, a boundary-strip construction gives a nonoptimal O((kR)−1/3)O((kR)^{-1/3}) error at fixed stack parameters. An ordered planar-design, positive-loss and aperture limit yields compact two-layer local nonmagnetic dielectrics with fixed upper material caps and absorption asymmetry arbitrarily close to one; the total extinction is not prescribed in this construction. Separately, we derive the sharp dimensional absorption-difference bound for complete reciprocal electric–magnetic dipole models and exhibit exactly radiation-dressed equality tensors. The dipole model has a finite directivity ceiling that does not apply to unrestricted compact bodies. These ordinary analytic results distinguish physical material truncation from computational windowing and point-polarizability feasibility from finite-dielectric realization. No joint resource limit, bandwidth bound or complete formal-verification claim is made.

1 Introduction and scope

Reciprocity equates the extinction cross sections of time-reversed incident waves while permitting different absorption and scattering [1]. Fundamental bounds on this redistribution motivate Open Question 7 of Miller and Monticone [2]. The normalization and physical class matter: a compact-body cross section has units of area, an infinite slab is described by power densities per transverse area, and a point-dipole model has its own finite-dimensional radiation constraint.

We address this distinction through two complementary analyses. The first proves localization of the actual Maxwell cross sections for literal material truncations of a fixed strictly absorbing planar stack. The volume-current estimate controls both incidences. Energy balance then controls the complete scattering integral. A square-aperture construction gives an explicit, nonoptimal convergence exponent. The second treats complete electric–magnetic dipole polarizabilities with radiation reaction, exhibiting the distinction between a sharp point-model bound and realization by a local finite dielectric.

The unrestricted compact unit supremum is included as an application of the localization theorem. It overlaps the stronger exact prescribed-extinction result in the companion manuscript [3]: that result implies the unrestricted supremum for the same two-layer class. Here extinction is allowed to vary and fixed-stack localization is proved directly, with an explicit square-aperture rate. Prescribing total extinction during aperture growth requires further uniformity at vanishing physical coupling; it is not obtained by the fixed-stack limit alone. No verification record from the companion is transferred to the present proofs.

We distinguish these results from several established physical ingredients. Magnetoelectric optical theorems and inverse-polarizability radiation dressing are developed by Sersic and coauthors [4]. Reciprocal particle asymmetry and its nonorthogonal-mode mechanism are studied by Hopkins and coauthors [5]; Mann and Alù’s conference abstract announces maximal directional absorption in bianisotropic dipolar particles [6]. Antenna efficiency and optical-theorem bounds identify directivity-dependent limitations [7, 8]. Polarization conversion with selective attenuation is established [9, 10], and metasurface directional absorption has related model-specific bounds [11].

The passage from a planar stack to a finite material aperture also has relevant precedents. Gao, Yang and Kattawar analyze polarized extinction of large-aspect-ratio plates [12]. Windowed Green-function methods treat layered backgrounds [13, 14], while volume-integral formulations supply the singular-kernel and radiation framework [15, 16]. Computational windowing of an infinite-interface equation and truncation of the physical material are different operations. Our smooth windows modify only trial polarizations; the final material aperture remains sharp. These comparisons do not establish first priority for the localization theorem or dipole equality family.

Stored energy and damping dissipation are nonnegative; summing over the real principal axes preserves locality and reciprocity. This compatibility observation does not impose upper caps or performance over a finite band.

The dipole bound is sharp within a complete radiation-consistent point model, and its near-unit sequence has vanishing total extinction. The compact construction has no such restriction: its selected aperture can grow after the planar design is fixed. The localization theorem proves all three area-normalized cross-section limits and a fixed-stack square-aperture upper error. It does not give an optimal global resource cost, a rate uniform as loss vanishes, or a finite-bandwidth law. Sharp bounded-size and coercive-loss resource optima remain separate problems.

The unrestricted compact supremum is weaker than the companion prescribed-extinction theorem. The distinctive analyses presented here are the complete dipole equality tensors and radiation removal, the general fixed-stack material-aperture passage, and its explicit square-window rate. Their ordinary proofs stand independently of numerical experiments.
