Abstract

Reciprocity equates the extinction cross sections for opposite real linearly polarized plane waves, while permitting different absorption and scattering. At a fixed vacuum wavenumber k>0k>0, we prove that the supremum of the absorption difference divided by common extinction is one at every exactly prescribed extinction E*>0E_*>0. The construction uses finite compact two-layer local anisotropic nonmagnetic dielectrics satisfying arbitrary fixed caps P>1P>1 and Q>0Q>0, with I≼Re⁡Hε≼PII\preceq\operatorname{Re}_{\!H}\varepsilon\preceq PI and 0≺Im⁡Hε≼QI0\prec\operatorname{Im}_{\!H}\varepsilon\preceq QI in each layer. Every individual body has asymmetry strictly below one. The key step is an aperture limit uniform after factoring out the squared sine of the material rotation angle, including its zero-angle auxiliary limit. This permits the coupling angle to decrease as the aperture grows while extinction remains fixed. Positive isotropic loss is then added, and continuity permits exact retuning to the target. The approximating sequence has no common geometric resource bound or positive loss floor, and the main theorem is monochromatic. The contribution is the controlled compact Maxwell realization at prescribed extinction. An accompanying Lean 4 formalization verifies the main single-frequency existence and supremum statements and physical power identities; its verification and review scope are specified separately. A separately labeled candidate proof proposes the same unit worst-frequency supremum on any prescribed finite positive-frequency band, including an exact extinction floor attained at the selected interior frequency. This broadband extension is not covered by the existing formalization or external human peer review.

1 Introduction and relation to OQ7

Open Question 7 in the tutorial of Miller and Monticone asks for fundamental limits on the redistribution between absorption and scattering under time-reversed illumination [1]. A normalization, illumination class, and resource class are needed to turn that question into a theorem. Here the normalization is the common dimensional plane-wave extinction, the illumination pair is opposite real linear polarization in vacuum, and the objects are local reciprocal dielectric bodies at one frequency.

The result is an existence and supremum theorem. It states that a finite object can have exactly any prescribed positive extinction and an absorption difference arbitrarily close to that extinction, while its real permittivity and loss obey fixed pointwise upper bounds. The main theorem establishes neither a finite-resource optimum nor finite-bandwidth performance. Section 9.1 separately presents a candidate broadband extension and its additional analytical dependencies. The same material tensor is used in both experiments, and scattering includes the whole outgoing sphere and both polarizations.

At a fixed coupling angle, enlarging a planar-like aperture increases extinction proportionally to area. A fixed-stack aperture limit therefore does not suffice to prescribe total extinction. The exact rotation factorization and uniform normalized aperture theorem below permit the coupling angle to decrease while the aperture grows. Green-operator and localization arguments justify this passage for actual finite Maxwell bodies. Dipole optimization, scalar thickness laws, fixed-resource bounds, and quartic small-size results have different hypotheses and are outside the present proof.

2 Prior work and the precise contribution

Reciprocal extinction equality and directional absorption are established facts. Sounas and Alù derived opposite-incidence extinction symmetry and its implications for scattering manipulation [2]. Hopkins and coauthors obtained directional absorption and scattering at equal extinction through modal interference in reciprocal particles [3]. Power conservation gives the passive bound used here, and the optical theorem gives strict nonattainment for compact bodies.

Polarization conversion combined with selective attenuation also has established precedents. Armitage develops reciprocity constraints for Jones matrices and discusses a polarizer–waveplate example [4]. Khurgin and coauthors demonstrate reciprocal asymmetric polarization switching using selective attenuation and imperfect waveplates, including passive experiments [5]. Thin bianisotropic absorbers and metasurfaces provide further examples of distinct opposite-side responses [6, 7]. Máñez-Espina and coauthors derive maximum directional absorptance difference in a reciprocal bianisotropic metasurface model and verify its predictions with full-wave simulations [8]. Kononchuk and coauthors combine selective attenuation with magnetic Faraday rotation in a layered-sheet isolator [9]; its complete device is magnetically nonreciprocal. These constructions must be compared with local nonmagnetic dielectric volumes under their respective constitutive and geometric assumptions.

Efficiency and response bounds clarify the resource requirements. Andersen and Frandsen show that receiving-antenna absorption divided by absorption plus total scattering can approach one at a directivity-dependent cost [10]. Schab and collaborators formulate absorption–scattering tradeoffs with prescribed losses and material parameters [11], while Khurgin and Sun analyze loss scaling in resonant metal–dielectric structures [12]. Sounas and Alù derive multiport and free-space response bounds, including a stated extension to loss through additional channels [13]. These results constrain related responses but do not themselves construct the present opposite-incidence family at every prescribed extinction under arbitrary fixed dielectric caps.

For the finite-to-planar passage, Gao, Yang, and Kattawar study polarized extinction of plates with large aspect ratios [14]. Gustafsson and coauthors derive planar extinction and scattered-transmission identities for periodic slabs [15]. Windowed Green-function methods treat layered backgrounds [16, 17], and volume-integral formulations supply the singular-kernel and radiation framework [18, 19]. A computational window in an infinite-interface equation differs from literal truncation of a material body. Here smooth windows enter only trial currents; the final material aperture remains sharp.

Mann and Alù’s conference abstract announces maximal directional absorption for bianisotropic dipolar particles [20]. It is direct prior art for the qualitative extremal phenomenon, although its full equations, normalization and possible equivalence to a compact dielectric realization remain unverified. The contribution established here is the uniform normalized compact-aperture limit at vanishing physical coupling, combined with exact finite-object extinction tuning and positive-loss regularization within arbitrary fixed upper material caps. These results distinguish the quantified theorem from the preceding mechanisms and bounds; they do not establish first priority.

6 Planar dielectric construction and rotation factorization

The mechanism combines selective absorption with polarization conversion. In the auxiliary infinite planar model with zero plate loss, the absorber acts only on the xx component and is vacuum-equivalent for yy polarization, while the plate is a reflectionless half-wave plate. From the absorber side, incident yy polarization passes through the absorber before conversion in transmission. From the plate side, conversion occurs first, so an xx component reaches the absorber. The ideal plate produces no backward reflection; any reflection of the stack originates at the absorber, and scattering also includes the change in transmission relative to vacuum. This auxiliary model supplies intuition and a limiting target. The final objects are finite and compact, with positive active plate loss and added positive isotropic loss in both layers, followed by exact extinction retuning.
