Abstract

Reciprocity equates extinction under time-reversed illumination but permits unequal absorption. We quantify this asymmetry in two monochromatic settings. For reciprocal passive channel operators with unit incident power, we determine the sharp absorption-difference bound at prescribed common extinction and prescribed overlap between an input and its background time reverse. The resulting three-branch envelope has an explicit positive-matrix certificate and attainable equality operators. For finite scalar dielectric multilayers with fixed upper material caps and fixed integrated loss, we prove a sharp inverse-square approach to the established two-port ceiling at exactly prescribed transmission and mean absorption in its strict active branch. The matching construction uses thin lossy slices near standing-wave nodes and finite bounded-index dielectric matching layers. An exact two-layer anisotropic planar construction shows why a scalar loss-budget determinant floor does not extend to semidefinite tensor loss; finite-power beams retain its fixed-extinction supremum. The channel statements use dimensionless power normalization, whereas the thickness theorem concerns a specified one-dimensional material class. Neither the abstract equality operators nor the planar examples establish compact three-dimensional Maxwell realization at nonzero prescribed illumination overlap. Layer count and minimum feature size are unrestricted, and no finite-bandwidth conclusion is asserted.

1 Introduction

Lorentz reciprocity constrains directional optical response without requiring absorption to be direction independent. In particular, a reciprocal object has equal extinction for time-reversed incident waves, although absorption and scattering can exchange their contributions [1]. Open Question 7 in the tutorial of Miller and Monticone asks for fundamental bounds on this absorption–scattering asymmetry [2]. To formulate a bound, we must specify the power normalization, the pair of illuminations and the allowed material resources. We distinguish a complete-channel problem with finite incident power from a scalar multilayer problem with a fixed material-loss budget.

Our first main result is a sharp joint channel envelope at fixed common extinction XX and fixed overlap ρ\rho between an input and its background time reverse. The familiar single-input passivity parabola and the overlap bound are separately sharp, but their minimum need not be jointly attainable. We determine the intervening branch using an explicit positive-matrix certificate and construct equality operators in two or three channels. This is an abstract operator result; physical realization of its equality operators at nonzero ρ\rho is a separate problem.

Our second main result is a sharp material-resource asymptotic for scalar dielectric multilayers. At fixed transmitted power TT, mean absorptance aa, integrated imaginary permittivity MM and upper material caps, the optimal absorption difference approaches the established two-port ceiling with a deficit proportional to the inverse square of total thickness. We prove both the uniform lower bound and its matching coefficient, while retaining exactly the prescribed T,a,MT,a,M. The construction permits arbitrarily many layers and shrinking lossy slices. It is therefore distinct from optimization with a fixed layer count, minimum feature size or material catalogue.

Several ingredients of these results have substantial precedents. Guo and Fan characterize reciprocal reflection feasibility and related multimode passivity constraints [3, 4]; Rollett’s invariant supplies the coating-invariant two-port quantity used below [5]. Single-frequency material-dependent bounds are established in photonics [6], and quantitative observability has related Gramian and small-window bounds [7, 8, 9]. Loss placed near standing-wave minima is the established optical Borrmann mechanism [10, 11]. Our thickness statement concerns a variable complex spatial Helmholtz equation, an all-profile bound and an exact-target leading coefficient.

For channels, the passivity envelope is a direct consequence of the optical-theorem absorption–scattering constraint [12]. Existing reciprocity and time-reversal bounds [13, 14], coherent-control results [15], and quantum information–disturbance tradeoffs [16, 17] provide relevant comparisons. The present joint problem fixes a coherent forward overlap of a transpose-symmetric contraction and the difference of two output powers. These hypotheses differ from the inspected statements; an equivalent prior formulation has not been excluded. We make no claim of first priority. Reciprocal polarization conversion with selective attenuation is also established [18, 19, 20], as is directional absorption in bianisotropic metasurfaces [21]. The planar construction in Section 7 uses that mechanism to exhibit the limits of transferring scalar resource bounds to tensor materials.

Sections 2–4 establish the scalar thickness result. Sections 5–6 establish the channel envelope. Section 7 supplies the planar Maxwell construction and its finite-power extension. All proofs are ordinary analytic proofs; formal-verification scope is stated separately in Section 8.

8 Scope, verification and conclusions

The sharp thickness law applies to local scalar multilayers at normal incidence with the fixed pointwise caps and integrated loss in (7). It excludes T=0T=0, a=0a=0, a=(1−T2)/2a=(1-T^2)/2 and P=1P=1, where the matching Jacobian or active-branch deficit can degenerate. It establishes an asymptotic optimum, not the exact optimizer at finite thickness or a minimum layer count. Lossy slices shrink and layer count grows; finite feature size and a finite catalogue of dispersive materials require different hypotheses.

The joint channel theorem is sharp for reciprocal contractions at fixed dimensionless extinction and input overlap. Its equality operators are explicitly constructed, but no Maxwell equality realization at nonzero prescribed overlap is supplied. The planar construction has laterally infinite material extent even for finite-power beams. It establishes neither compact-body cross-section sharpness nor a fixed-resource coercive tensor-loss theorem. All results are monochromatic; they impose no bandwidth constraint.

It also proves the actual tensor-layer construction and the finite-power beam construction using the normalized uniform disk aperture specified above. The latter includes the physical Fourier normalization, regional Maxwell equations, all interfaces, incoming and outgoing expansions, physical net flux and exact common-extinction retuning.
