Broadband candidate proof · 17 October 2026

A candidate proof on a prescribed band

A separate candidate proof asks whether the single-frequency result extends to a fixed positive-frequency band B. It proposes one finite, passive, causal two-layer body whose extinction satisfies X(center) = E* = min_B X at a chosen interior frequency, with strict inequality elsewhere in B, while inf_B Γ is arbitrarily close to 1. The proposed supremum is one and is not attained; the extinction spectrum is not exactly flat.

This is a CANDIDATE proof: the complete broadband existence claim has not been formally verified or independently reviewed. It inherits the earlier weak-Maxwell/operator framework and the strict-floor note’s C0 frequency-uniform aperture theorem. Material caps apply bandwise; the construction imposes no common geometry or resource bound, resonance-linewidth floor, or positive material-loss floor. Fixed-resource optima and an all-isotropic construction remain open.

Here X is the extinction cross section: the total incident power removed by absorption and scattering, normalized by incident intensity. Γ is the absolute absorption difference between opposite illuminations divided by their common extinction. The infimum asks for the smallest Γ across the entire band.

The boundary note extends the earlier strict-floor candidate (0 < E_min < E*). Their numerical checks are limited diagnostics, not continuum-band certificates or finite-device simulations. The original research status and verification records are preserved in the source downloads.
