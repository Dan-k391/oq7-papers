# Reference-derived editorial framework (29 October 2026)

The observations below are based on inspected passages in all seven papers. They are communication principles, not scientific evidence for the manuscript's claims, and do not reproduce distinctive prose. This was a focused study, not an exhaustive line-by-line audit of every reference.

1. Preskill (2018), abstract and sections 1–3: Establish the physical opportunity, practical obstacle, central assessment and scope before technical detail. Separate established capability from anticipated applications. https://arxiv.org/html/1801.00862
2. Feynman (1948), sections 1–6 of the article reproduced in Feynman's Thesis, printed pages 71 onward: Explain the advantage of a reformulation; begin with alternatives and amplitude addition; develop discretized paths into a postulate; resolve the initially ambiguous path sum with a limiting prescription; explain wavefunctions through their role in prediction; motivate the short-time expansion by cancellation before deriving the familiar equation. Transfer the sequence physical construction → precise definition → controlled calculation → familiar consequence. The Caltech PDF redirect failed and the standalone Washington PDF was scanned, but the reproduced article was readable here: https://faculty.washington.edu/seattle/physics541/%202010-reading/Feynman-thesis.pdf
3. Mermin (1998), sections I–III: Introduce the central idea, define ambiguous terms operationally, provide concrete subsystem examples, specify formal conditions and explicitly distinguish unresolved issues from the proposed account. Transfer clarity and boundary-setting, not the interpretational thesis. https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9801057
4. LIGO/Virgo (2016), abstract, section II/Figure 1, sections IV–V: Present event, measured signal, statistical evidence, inferred quantities and interval convention distinctly. Captions explain processing, alignment, comparisons, uncertainties and residuals. Connect conclusions to instrumental validation and complementary searches. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.03837
5. EHT (2019), abstract, sections 1 and 4: Separate reconstructed morphology from model-assisted inference; identify factors complicating a simple observable-to-parameter relation; explain cross-checks between calibration pipelines and observing epochs. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.11238
6. Carroll (2001 journal reference; inspected arXiv version dated 2000), sections 1.1–1.3: Motivate the physical question, state conventions and symmetry assumptions, introduce variables with the equations, and interpret their consequences before introducing the next difficulty. https://arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0004075
7. Anderson (1972), pages 393–394: Distinguish a premise from an unjustified converse, anticipate likely misunderstandings, and develop a general argument from a concrete molecular example; mark simplifications and speculative extensions. https://www.rpgroup.caltech.edu/embl_pboc_2023/assets/pdfs/anderson1972.pdf

Operational framework: A section should establish its question and motivation, explain how the method addresses it, define objects and assumptions, develop the calculation, interpret the result and explain the next step. Major equations need both a purpose and a consequence. Distinguish measured/calculated quantities from inferred physical conclusions. Abstracts should contain the problem, method, principal result and supported significance. Captions should identify quantities, processing, comparisons and uncertainty.

Editorial invariant: Preserve equations, numbers, assumptions, definitions, citations and conclusions. Flag suspected scientific problems separately; do not silently repair them.
