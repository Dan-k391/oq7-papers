## Paper B: highest-priority localization argument

**Finding:** no fatal analytical error found under the exact fixed-profile, strictly positive-loss hypotheses of Theorem 3.1. The result has an ordinary analytical proof. It is not currently a kernel-verified Maxwell theorem.

1. The zero-extended Newtonian part of the electric Green operator has Fourier multiplier `-ξξᵀ/|ξ|²`. This includes its local distributional term and is bounded on full-space L². On a bounded difference set the Hessian of `g_k-g_0` and the term `k²g_k` have locally integrable, O(1/r) singularities. Schur estimates therefore give the required bounded operator for each finite aperture. This does **not** provide an aperture-uniform Green norm.
2. The identity `-Im χ⁻¹ = χ⁻† (Im χ) χ⁻¹` gives `W ≥ δ/K² I` when `Im χ ≥ δI` and `||χ|| ≤ K`. Radiation has nonnegative imaginary quadratic form. Thus the inverse bound for `χ⁻¹-T_R` follows from coercivity of `i⟨·,L_R·⟩`, with constant `c=δ/K²` independent of R. Boundedness of the form is needed only separately for each R. Complex Lax-Milgram then proves surjectivity as well as injectivity; a lower norm estimate alone would not suffice.
3. The convolution construction gives locally L² electric fields and locally H¹ curls, hence outgoing weak Maxwell fields. Conversely subtracting that convolution from any outgoing weak solution leaves an entire homogeneous vacuum Maxwell solution; its divergence is zero and its components solve Helmholtz. Outgoing uniqueness (Rellich plus exterior unique continuation, or the entire spherical-harmonic expansion) makes the difference zero. This direct argument covers the tensor hypothesis; the cited 2018 article's isotropic hypothesis alone does not.
4. With `β=(k²-|κ|²)^(1/2)` on the outgoing branch, `Gκ=i exp(iβ|s|)/(2β)` has `∂s²Gκ=-β²Gκ-δ₀`. The longitudinal fiber is `|κ|²Gκ*-I`, not just its ordinary kernel. Transverse-mixed fibers have the derivative of Gκ. These formulas give a norm bounded by `C(1+|κ|+(k²+|κ|²)/|β|)` and a Lipschitz difference at κ=0. The circle singularity is inverse square root in radial distance: locally L¹, generally not L². A global transverse Plancherel estimate there would be invalid. The manuscript uses L¹ there.
5. For a fixed smooth interior window g(x/R), the low-frequency commutator is O(1) by Hilbert-valued Plancherel and `||∇g_R||₂=||∇g||₂`. At high frequency its Fourier L¹ norm is `O(R^(2-N))` for N>3. The grazing-circle singularity is integrable in this estimate. Restriction to a transverse set of area O(R²) gives high-frequency local L² norm `O(R^(3-N))`. Therefore the residual is o(R). No global L² assertion about the outgoing radiating field is required.
6. The residual/current comparison is
   `||P_R-q|| ≤ R(c⁻¹||f||+||q||)||1-g||_{L²(Ω)} + c⁻¹||H_R||`.
   First let R tend to infinity with g fixed, then approximate 1 by smooth compactly supported windows in Ω. This proves o(R). It is not a limit uniform in vanishing δ or increasing L.
7. Cauchy-Schwarz converts the current error into o(R²) extinction error. The bounded quadratic absorption form gives o(R²) absorption error. Exact energy balance gives the **total** scattering error as their difference. This retains forward diffraction, edge radiation, cross-polarization, and the entire sphere, without requiring convergence of individual angular amplitudes.
8. The square-window estimates are `||(1-g)||₂=O((Rw)^(1/2))`, `||∇g||₂=O((R/w)^(1/2))`, and fifth-derivative L¹ norms `O(Rw⁻⁴)`. Thus the current error is bounded by fixed-stack constants times `(Rw)^(1/2)+(R/w)^(1/2)+R²w⁻⁴`. Choosing `w=k⁻¹(kR)^(1/3)` gives current error O((kR)^(2/3)) with dimensional constants and area-normalized observable error O((kR)^(-1/3)). Different terms have different units unless their constants carry the appropriate powers of k. This is an expository clarification, not a rate counterexample.

## Paper B: compact construction and dipoles

The exact wave plate diagonalizes a real symmetric involution V. For arbitrary P>1, an integer j can be chosen with `(2j/(2j-1))²≤P`; the eigenphases are consecutive integer multiples of π, so both eigenpolarizations are exactly reflectionless. Its reference-normalized Jones transmission is V. The absorbing layer's y polarization is vacuum; reversed y illumination acquires an absorbing x component. The scalar formulas have nonzero denominators because both the interface reflection and the internal propagation factor have magnitude less than one.

The sequence `η=1/m`, `kh=m²`, `t=1-1/m` has vanishing interface mismatch and diverging attenuation, hence absorptance of x tends to one and planar Γ tends to one. This is an infinite planar result only. For each chosen finite stack, transfer-matrix continuity at δ=0 permits addition of `iδI` with δ>0. The outgoing boundary problem at δ=0 is invertible by passive flux balance and ODE uniqueness; susceptibility inversion at δ=0 is neither needed nor valid. Choose η and δ below Q in order. Only after both are fixed apply localization and choose a finite R. This establishes the compact sequence while allowing inverse constants and geometrical size to grow between members. It works for every fixed P>1, Q>0.

The upper bound Γ≤1 follows from nonnegative absorption/scattering and equal extinction. For a regular individual compact object, equality would force vanishing far-field scattering in one direction with positive extinction. Continuity of its far field plus the optical theorem rules this out. Strictly absorbing constructed bodies also have positive absorption for both nonzero incident fields.

The complete electric-magnetic dipole radiation integral includes transverse electric and magnetic squares and an odd cross term whose sphere integral vanishes. With `A=k³α/(6π)`, it yields scattering `6π||Af||²/k²`. The passive reciprocal dipole problem becomes a symmetric contraction after the stated magnetic-coordinate rotation. Its parabola is an algebraic passivity consequence; the displayed rank-one absorption defect is consistent. Its supremum-one limit has extinction tending to zero and is not a local dielectric realization proof or a substitute for Theorem 3.11.

## Paper A: channel results and all three branches of Theorem 7.1

The single-output inequality follows from `|⟨s₀,s-s₀⟩|≤||s-s₀||`: scattering is at least X²/4, so absorption is at most X-X²/4. Equal extinction for reciprocal transpose-symmetric S and its background-reversed input then gives the absorption-difference bound. Its three-channel Gram-defect witness realizes every X in [0,4]. This is abstract channel algebra.

For scalar two ports, reverse-triangle reduction to a real contraction preserves PSD defect. Writing its eigenvalues w±q yields `w+q≤1`; optimizing the fixed-extinction envelope gives `2q²-q-τ²=0`. The normalized maximum satisfies `1-q-q²=0`, giving `sqrt(10sqrt(5)-22)`. Reverse-triangle saturation imposes the stated reflection phases. The finite-thickness phase-control slab changes the vacuum-relative transmission phase; an appended vacuum spacer cannot do that. Theorem 6.4 has the same exact finite planar construction as B3.9. Corollary 6.5 uses a nonsingular fixed-axis power-normalized vacuum basis near κ=0, continuity of finite matrix exponentials and the scattering solve, then an IVT angle retuning. Its limits are diagonal over separately fixed slabs and do not establish compact bodies.

For Theorem 7.1, a background-real orthonormal basis and global input phase give `u=pe₁+iqe₂` with p²=(1+ρ)/2 and q²=(1-ρ)/2. A unitary congruence gives real inputs a,b=Ja and reference J=diag(1,-1). Realification only discards imaginary/output components, so the inequality is valid even with auxiliary channels. Extinction gives tr(WB); the contrast is `2sqrt(1-ρ²)|Q₁₂|`. The two sign operations used to take r,h≥0 preserve both feasible constraints.

* First branch: Q₁₂≤1/2, with equality only at one of the two rank-one ±45° projectors. Its kernel then lies in ker B, forcing B=αP and r≤ρ/2. The complex phase projection witness realizes every r in that interval.
* Middle branch: in B₀'s eigenbasis, the displayed Z has positive first diagonal and determinant `D(ρ-c)/(16c(1-c²))>0`. Direct substitution gives `B₀Z+ZB₀=W` and `H=Z-νE` rank-one PSD supported on the eigenvalue-one subspace. Expanding `tr((B-B₀)Z(B-B₀))` and the two PSD-budget trace products gives `r≤r₀+ν(h-h₀)`.
