Abstract
The requested fixed-transmission, fixed-mean-absorption optimization for a passive recip-
rocal two-port is established: it is a direct slice of the complete reflection-feasibility theorem
of Guo and Fan (2022). We give its exact feasible region, optimum, and all equality cases.
We then study a strictly narrower Maxwell problem: finite nonmagnetic dielectric multilayers
at one frequency, with1≤Reε≤P, 0≤Imε≤Q, fixed integrated lossM, and thickness at
most L. In the strict active branch of the matrix optimum, we prove an exact inverse-square
asymptotic for its unavoidable absorption-asymmetry deficit, including the optimal leading
constant and exact fixed transmission and mean absorption. The lower bound is uniform over
arbitrary finite layer counts; the upper bound is realized asymptotically by ordinary dielectric
layers. Two independent mathematical agent reviews and independent transfer calculations
found no obstruction. Verdict: KNOWN for the matrix problem and the two-port in-
variant;CONDITIONAL for the priority of the proved material-constrained asymptotic;
UNRESOLVEDfor general three-dimensional OQ7 and finite-bandwidth extensions. No
Lean kernel verification was obtained.
1 Novelty check before theorem development
The starting question is OQ7 in Section 4.1 of Miller and Monticone’s tutorial [1]. A two-port
specialization does not by itself solve the general optical cross-section question. Throughout this
note, absorption and transmission are power fractions for a pair of power-normalized ports. For
reciprocal two-port systems,
AL−AR =|rR|2−|rL|2.
Thus the same difference measures the opposite imbalance in reflected power. For an extended
free-space object, identifying all scattered power with one reflection channel would require
additional assumptions.

7.2 What remains unresolved
The exact optimal envelope at every finiteL, the complete set of asymptotically optimizing
profiles, and a necessary minimum layer count remain open in this investigation. The strict
limits T = 0, a = 0, anda = (1−T 2)/2, as well as the material caseP = 1, are excluded from
Theorem 5.1; its Jacobian or envelope arguments can degenerate there. A bound valid with
a fixed positive minimum feature size or a finite catalogue of dispersive materials would be a
different realizability problem.
The present result is monochromatic. The hypotheses prescribe material values at one frequency
and do not specify dispersion over a frequency interval, so they do not establish a nontrivial
finite-bandwidth or causality tradeoff. General vector, oblique-incidence, multipolar, or three-
dimensional absorption–scattering asymmetry is likewise not settled.
