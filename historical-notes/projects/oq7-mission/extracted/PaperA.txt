1 Novelty check before theorem development
The starting question is OQ7 in Section 4.1 of Miller and Monticone’s tutorial [1]. A two-port
specialization does not by itself solve the general optical cross-section question. In Sections 2–5,
absorption and transmission are power fractions for a pair of power-normalized ports. For
reciprocal two-port systems,
AL−AR =|rR|2−|rL|2.
Thus the same difference measures the opposite imbalance in reflected power. For an extended
free-space object, identifying all scattered power with one reflection channel would require
additional assumptions.

1.3 Additional priority checks for the generalization
The channel inequality in Section 6 follows from the optical-theorem passivity disk underlying
absorption–scattering tradeoffs, including Fleury, Soric and Alù [18]. Its two-illumination form
is not claimed as an independent discovery. The sharper scalar two-port optimizations are
consequences of Guo–Fan reflection feasibility, rather than new matrix constraints.
Polarization conversion is also an established source of directional power asymmetry. Armitage
[19], Eq. (27), gives reversed Jones transmission as the transpose; the example around Eq. (34)
explicitly combines a polarizer with a rotated wave plate. Potton’s earlier review [20], Section 5,
treats reciprocal polarizing stacks. Our exact Maxwell construction supplies finite tensor coeffi-
cients, interface matching, a limiting sequence, and fixed-extinction sharpness. It is a rigorous
synthesis of known mechanisms. The targeted search did not locate the identical constructive
theorem, but that does not establish novelty or warrant labeling it NEW.
Guo and Fan’s multimode work [9] completely characterizes the joint eigenvalues of transmission,
reflection and absorption for a specified input side through Horn inequalities. It does not impose
reciprocity on the full two-sided system and explicitly identifies reciprocal refinements as further
work. It should neither be rediscovered nor represented as solving all reversed-pair Maxwell
questions. A source-by-source comparison, including the limitations of full-text retrieval for
Sounas–Alù (2014), Potton (2004), and Martin (1962), is supplied in the reproducibility archive.

the exact equation

within the stated caps, rather than a fixed catalogue of measured materials.

B.4 Reproducibility and preservation record
The source package includes the original PDF and original reproducibility archive without
modification, a separate revised manuscript, and a hash ledger. The original numerical scripts
run under Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.3.5, SciPy 1.17.0, and Matplotlib 3.10.8. Exact polynomial
checks also use SymPy 1.13.3. The four reproduced JSON files are byte-identical to their originals;
the comparison is recorded inreproduction/comparison_with_originals.json.
The new experiments provide three independently written normal-incidence implementations:
a matrix exponential calculation, a closed scattering cascade, and a modal interface-continuity
solve. The beam check uses the oblique four-component Maxwell equations. Their outputs,
random seeds, optimization tolerances, and assertions remain in the scripts and JSON records.
Numerical calculations diagnose errors; none supplies an omitted analytic or formal proof.
From the extracted source root, the entry points are
python3 scripts/reproduce_numerics.py
cd lean
bash scripts/bootstrap-linux.sh
cd ../manuscript
latexmk -pdf -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error oq7_generalized.tex
The top-level README states dependency installation, alternative explicit build commands, file
layout, and the exact remaining formalization blockers. Build logs document both successful
checks and relevant failed approaches; a previous failed attempt is not represented as the final
status.
