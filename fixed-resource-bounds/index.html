Abstract

We bound the absolute absorption difference divided by common extinction for time-reversed plane waves incident on compact reciprocal dielectric bodies in vacuum. An integrated loss-weighted response budget ℳ\mathcal M gives the all-angle bound Γ≤k3ℳ/(6π+k3ℳ)\Gamma\leq k^3\mathcal M/(6\pi+k^3\mathcal M). For a bounded local response and support in a radius-aa ball, the complete electric-current radiation spectrum satisfies ‖ℛa‖≤ka\lVert \mathcal R_a\rVert\leq ka at every frequency. At positive required extinction, a resolvent of this operator supplies a strictly positive scattering floor, and an explicit optimizer proves the exact minimum of the stated current relaxation. A homogeneous, strictly lossy reciprocal anisotropic sphere has the exact-Maxwell expansion Γ=(160/2071)(ka)3+O((ka)4)\Gamma=(160/2071)(ka)^3+O((ka)^4) for a circular time-reversed polarization pair. This cubic order concerns the general complex-polarization class; opposite real linear polarizations admit a stronger quartic refinement. The response and efficiency principles have established precedents. The bounds here do not determine the sharp fixed-resource Maxwell supremum or establish that two relaxed optimizers can be realized by the same local material.

7 Extensions and hypothesis limitations

7.1 Isotropy and weaker loss assumptions

The response estimate includes arbitrary reciprocal local tensors and scalar isotropic media. For scalar ℑχ>0\Im\chi>0, η=|χ|2/ℑχ\eta=\lvert \chi\rvert^2/\Im\chi. The sphere construction is anisotropic; no matching isotropic cubic construction or isotropic optimum is supplied.

Condition (13) allows nonuniform or vanishing loss only when responding directions remain controlled. Positive trace loss, positive integrated loss or positivity on one polarization does not imply it. In particular, a lossless direction with nonzero susceptibility violates the finite response condition. A replacement compactness proof without this control would need uniform Maxwell resolvent estimates, an appropriate topology for geometry and coefficients, a justified homogenization closure and uniqueness for the limiting class. Weak coefficient convergence alone supplies none of these requirements.

7.4 Other channels, backgrounds and bandwidth

For a general incident electric field, the extinction estimate uses ∫Dη|f|2dx\int_D\eta\lvert f\rvert^2\,\mathrm{d}x in place of ℳ\mathcal M. A reciprocal pair with equal positive extracted power under one common normalization admits the same interval argument. Finite-power channel fractions and plane-wave cross sections have different units.

For waveguides or substrates, a complete positive radiation operator must include every open and guided outgoing channel. The abstract loss-metric reasoning can then apply, but the vacuum constants and ball spectrum do not transfer automatically. Uncounted outgoing channels cannot be identified with absorption.

All main results are single-frequency statements. If one dispersive body satisfies the stated hypotheses at every frequency in a band, the bounds apply pointwise and give the corresponding worst-case ceiling, including a strict gap under uniform versions of the fixed-resource hypotheses. They neither supply an additional constraint coupling frequencies through causality nor establish joint realizability of the frequencywise relaxed optimizers by one material law. A sharper causal bandwidth tradeoff and the sharp jointly attainable band optimum remain outside these results; matching a tensor at one frequency in (40) does not settle either question.

8 Relation to existing response bounds and remaining optimization

The integrated-response ceiling follows established material-response and radiation-efficiency inequalities [5, 6, 7, 8]. The sphere decomposition is also an established current-optimization tool [9]. Time-reversal response constraints require careful distinctions between loss hypotheses and normalization [12, 13]. We do not claim a universally new response principle or an optimal fixed-resource Maxwell ceiling.

The explicit all-order estimate and positive-extinction resolvent provide quantitative restrictions for the plane-wave pair. Exactness of the one-current relaxation does not imply joint realizability: the same local reciprocal constitutive tensor must generate both currents, and its reactive Maxwell constraints must also hold. Cross-source and 𝕋\mathbb T-operator frameworks supply antecedents for enforcing such compatibility [14, 15, 16].

The cubic construction establishes a general complex-polarization small-volume order. It makes no assertion about an optimal coefficient, an isotropic extremizer or a fixed-positive-extinction limit. The later real-linear quartic refinement concerns a narrower illumination class [11]. The remaining problem is to determine the jointly attainable resource-constrained supremum, with a compatible physical construction or a residual-gap proof. One-sided real caps and uncontrolled lossless responding sectors are separate unresolved classes.
