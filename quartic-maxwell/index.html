Abstract

For opposite real linearly polarized plane waves, a reciprocal dielectric has equal extinction but can have different absorption and total scattering. We bound the absolute absorption difference divided by common extinction for compact nonmagnetic local media with a susceptibility norm cap MM and a full tensor loss floor m>0m>0. An exact Maxwell inequality gives order (kR)4(kR)^4 uniformly over all positive-volume measurable supports inside a radius-RR ball and all measurable admissible tensor patterns, including patterns that vary with size. For a fixed rescaled design, two static volume-response moments determine the quartic coefficient, with an O((kR)6)O((kR)^6) remainder. Two finite dielectric spheres with susceptibilities iIiI and 2iI2iI give an explicit positive coefficient interval and a full-Maxwell remainder bound. A homogeneous unequal-sphere construction proves exponent sharpness for every 0<m≤M0<m\leq M. The asymmetry mechanism and fixed-smooth-shape quartic upper order have prior precedents. The results here concern the geometric uniformity, explicit constants and continuum coefficient certificates; they do not determine an optimal prefactor, an exact finite-size optimum or a fixed-positive-extinction small-size limit.

1 Scope and principal results

6 Relation to earlier formulas and physical limits

The fixed-smooth-shape quartic upper order is already implicit in full-Maxwell small-inclusion expansions. In particular, Ammari, Deng and Millien, Theorem 5.8 and Eq. (5.32) of the first arXiv version [5], give a leading electric and magnetic static polarization term and an O(δ4)O(\delta^4) field remainder. For a fixed smooth homogeneous reference body, fixed lossy scalar contrast and zero magnetic contrast, the leading magnetic term vanishes. Both real opposite electric plane waves have the same value at the shrinking center. Consequently their far fields have the same δ3\delta^3 term, so S+−S−=O(δ7)S_+-S_-=O(\delta^7), while positive static dissipation gives X=Θ(δ3)X=\Theta(\delta^3). We infer Γ=O(δ4)\Gamma=O(\delta^4) under these fixed-shape hypotheses. This inference does not attribute our all-measurable-support quantifier or constants to that theorem.

Hopkins and coauthors’ transverse two-dipole equations (12)–(15), and their opposite-linear-polarization example in Fig. 3, are direct mechanism precedents [2]. To compare coefficients, take static volume polarizabilities α1,α2\alpha_1,\alpha_2, unit center separation and transverse coupling g=−1/(4π)g=-1/(4\pi). Their scalar reduction has uniform- and affine-drive total moments P0=α1+α2+2gα1α2Δ,P1=α2−α12Δ,Δ=1−g2α1α2.P_0=\frac{\alpha_1+\alpha_2+2g\alpha_1\alpha_2}{\Delta},\qquad P_1=\frac{\alpha_2-\alpha_1}{2\Delta},\qquad \Delta=1-g^2\alpha_1\alpha_2. Our algebraic comparison gives ℑ(P0*P1)=ℑ(α1*α2)+g(|α1|2ℑα2−|α2|2ℑα1)|Δ|2.\Im(P_0^*P_1)= \frac{\Im(\alpha_1^*\alpha_2) +g(\lvert \alpha_1\rvert^2\Im\alpha_2-\lvert \alpha_2\rvert^2\Im\alpha_1)}{\lvert \Delta\rvert^2}. For one material, αj=vjβ\alpha_j=v_j\beta and v2>v1v_2>v_1, this is −gv1v2(v2−v1)|β|2ℑβ|1−g2v1v2β2|2>0.\frac{-gv_1v_2(v_2-v_1)\lvert \beta\rvert^2\Im\beta} {\lvert 1-g^2v_1v_2\beta^2\rvert^2}>0. The leading homogeneous interaction sign is thus already present in the old point model. Sections 4–5 supply controlled finite-sphere statements, rather than a new two-dipole effect. Decreasing tt alone does not remove a fixed static multipole error for a fixed radius-to-gap ratio.

The contribution is the explicit inequality uniform over arbitrary measurable supports and capped measurable tensors, together with a static moment formula and quantitative continuum certificates for nonzero coefficients. No first-priority claim is made for the precise uniform theorem. The fixed-frequency hypotheses permit passive causal realizations of the displayed examples: for any γ>0\gamma>0 and operating frequency ω0>0\omega_0>0, χj(ω)=jγω0ω02−ω2−iγωI,j=1,2,\chi_j(\omega)=\frac{j\gamma\omega_0}{\omega_0^2-\omega^2-i\gamma\omega}I, \qquad j=1,2,(60) has poles in the lower half-plane, positive imaginary part for positive frequency, and χj(ω0)=ijI\chi_j(\omega_0)=ijI. Replacing jj by μ\mu realizes the homogeneous example. The geometry shrinks while those material functions stay fixed; no broadband loss floor or bandwidth optimum is implied.

A nonmagnetic constitutive law does not omit emergent magnetic multipoles of dielectric currents. Conversely, magnetic or bianisotropic constitutive response, spatial dispersion, a loss floor only on selected components, general elliptical illumination, or caps that diverge as size shrinks are outside these theorems. Nor do the theorems address a fixed-positive-extinction shrinking-object family: indeed X≤kρVX\leq k\rho V follows directly from ‖Tk‖≤ρ\lVert T_k\rVert\leq\rho, so X→0X\to0 uniformly as R→0R\to0 at fixed k,m,Mk,m,M.

7 Conclusion

A susceptibility cap and full tensor loss floor give a quartic upper bound for real opposite plane waves, uniformly over measurable geometries and material patterns. Two finite-sphere certificates show that this exponent occurs in the exact Maxwell problem, including within every resource class using one homogeneous material. The coefficient formula and explicit remainder control this assertion beyond a point-particle model. The optimal coefficient, the finite-size joint optimum and any additional fixed-positive-extinction or bandwidth requirement remain separate problems.
